Brief Community Eulogy for Ember

(I wrote this on behalf of her chosen family)

Ember Marí Padró y Pereira was a beloved member of our community and family. She passed away suddenly and unexpectedly in the early hours of November 1st after celebrating Halloween with loved ones at karaoke night. She was incredibly intelligent, beautiful, and creative. She worked tirelessly to make this cruel world better for others and sacrificed much of herself in that mission. She loved Transformers, Gunpla, D&D, comic books, One Piece, video games, music from the 00’s, and feisty little dogs. She loved us more unhesitatingly, consistently, sturdily, and deeply than many ever experienced before. The world is significantly worse without her. She left us far too soon, especially because she was meant to be immortal.

My personal eulogy for her is -- true to my usual form for deeply personal subjects -- much longer.

How Corry Met Ember

In or around 2016, I went on a few dates with someone awesome named Rhys. They weren't Rhys yet, and I wasn't who I am today yet either. We had a good couple dates before deciding to keep it platonic, and through that friendship I met several new friends including their best friend Ember.

At first Ember was just a really cool connection on social media. We had similar interests, aligned politics, and she was good at writing her thoughts in ways I felt were easy to understand. We chatted casually and followed each others' lives online from a few miles away.

In 2017, Wizards of the Coast did a special event at Comic Con where they released an exclusive set of Transformers Magic The Gathering cards, featuring Grimlok. Ember was going to ComicCon, but hadn't played Magic yet at the time. I asked if she'd buy me one if she could and I'd pay her back when I got it from her.

During the pandemic, I was on social media more often than before. Ember and I started chatting one-on-one more in addition to in the comments of each others' posts. She read my blog and I read all the Transformers comics she sent me digital copies of.

The first time I heard her voice was when I hosted a Zoom LEGO night where we all assembled our own kits over Zoom and chatted while we did. She was shy about showing her face, and kept the camera on her LEGO model.

Our First In-Person Meeting

One pandemic later, after we were all vaccinated, I started getting my Movie Crew back together in my new home in Emeryville. I don't remember which movie it was -- perhaps Dune in October, or Eternals in November?

Ember shows up with the Grimlock Magic The Gathering set she got me back in 2017 and I never expected to get at this point. And she didn't even let me Venmo her for it. Not even the $80 it cost her, let alone the current value of the set at the time, which was over $800. She was always incredibly generous, but this was the first time I really saw how effortlessly she gave and how she lit up when she could show her love that way.

Movies

Ember and I bonded a lot over seeing movies in theaters together. We loved hanging out after to talk about whatever we just watched. She liked every movie I hated, and thought the movies I loved were okay. We usually both liked whatever it was more after we talked about it. Or sometimes we both hated it. But the experience was always more fun together.

When I was in The Matrix Resurrections, she was one of the few people I confided in about my experience before my NDA ended because I knew how deeply she'd get my extreme level of excitement about it. She joined us at the local premiere and took photos of Bethany and I while declining to be in any herself.

I wanted to show her so many more movies. To be fair, it's probably best I didn't get around to traumatizing her with I Saw The TV Glow.

Mani-Pedis

Since before it was ever (consciously) a gender thing for me, I've been painting my nails and coaxing my friends of all genders to join me. Shortly after Ember and I began hanging out in real life and the world continued to reopen, I finally got her to join me.

The first time she went, she went with simple matte black nails, but she really loved them. Thinking back, it might've been my first time seeing her experience gender euphoria. Not long after, she started joining me whenever I went and even started getting more advanced cat's eye gels with awesome magnetic effects. Whenever she had a fresh mani, she'd proudly show them off to everyone, and I loved that for her.

It got to the point where I invited her to everything. She didn't always come, but we were always happy to see her when she did.

Slay The Spire

We had a Signal chat where Ember, Bethany, and I would do the Daily on Slay The Spire and compare our high scores. We talked trash, mostly about Bethany crushing us. And soon after the "crushes" Bethany was posting about expanded in meaning.

Our Relationship Develops

As we hung out more often, we got closer -- both metaphorically and physically. We started having one-on-one hangouts where we'd go to movies together, soak in the hot tub, or sit on the couch and game the way we did in our teens and college years. Over time, we'd scooch closer and closer on the couch and lean on each other more. Our hugs got tighter and lasted longer.

I grew really fond of "our cuddly friendship" as I described it to Bethany and my therapist. As we spent more time together, I began crushing on her a little, especially when she sang with us at karaoke. I was confused about this, because I've almost never held romantic or sexual feelings for men, even though I'd experimented here and there. As my feelings grew harder to ignore, I started to consider Ember one of my very few exceptions.

Deep Conversations

Sometimes Ember would come over for a gaming/watch session and we'd pause after like 10 minutes and spend hours talking instead as the screensaver bounced around on the TV.

Immortality

It seems so dark now, but we talked a lot about death, time, and immortality. I'd talk about how I didn't expect to live to 30 when I was younger, and how I still hope my death is quick, painless, and a surprise. She'd talk about how she was terrified of death and would let a vampire bite her immediately if she got the chance to be immortal.

I'd talk about how terrified I'd be of falling into a pit, sinking to the bottom of an ocean, or being imprisoned and tortured. My chronic pain issues have made me long for a permanent end to it. I'd talk about disabilities I'd not want to have forever, and all the stuff I learned about myself from reading Being Mortal -- a book I constantly recommended to her but she absolutely refused to check out. I talked about how horrible it'd be for me to live on while everyone I loved passed away before me. (a fear I'm validated in feeling right now)

She was undeterred. She said no matter what happened to her, she wanted to stay alive as long as possible. She'd want to live even if it meant being a Futurama-style head in a jar.

Gender Stuff

As she witnessed me transition, from chatting with me online about gender during the pandemic through every stage, she was more interested in the details than most. As I started dressing non-binary and disinhibiting in my voice, language, and mannerisms she expressed such pride and compersion for me.

We talked about growing up feeling like our consciousnesses were placed in the wrong bodies. As she got more comfortable talking about it, I'd remark on how she'd basically surrounded herself with queer and gender non-conforming people since before I met her. I'd nudge her every now and then attempting to crack her egg a little, but she'd refer to herself as the thousand-year egg -- an even more extreme version of the century egg in reference to the egg term in transgender discourse.

A few of her other friends and I nudged her from time to time, but respected her desire to stay in her shell. No one else can do that for us.

The One Topic Too Controversial for Us

Ember and I had exactly one conversation about it. We were so diametrically opposed on the topic that we both knew we should never bring it up again. I'm unabashedly and unapologetically pro, and she's staunchly and judgmentally con.

That topic was: Mayo.

We somehow got past our differences and let our alignment in 99% of other issues be our focus.

Our First Kiss

In early March 2025, while we were out at karaoke, Ember asked if she could kiss me. I knew she had a crush on Bethany, but didn't realize she also liked me that way. We were out at karaoke and she was feeling confident after rocking it on stage, like she regularly did. She had just kissed Bethany for the first time (with my encouragement) so I was happy for them. I didn't expect her to ask if she could kiss me too (with Bethany's encouragement).

I won't go into detail, but Ember was a great kisser. Better than her shyness about relationship stuff would ever make one expect.

A few weeks later, we had one of the conversations Ember dreaded. One where I told her how I felt about her and complimented her for several minutes straight. She had no problem saying that stuff to me, but got flustered whenever it was directed her way. But she deserved it, and she needed to get used to it if she was going to be around me loving her.

We talked about what we each wanted from our relationship. Neither of us was 100% sure. We both wanted to take it slow and try things without feeling committed to doing anything forever. Neither of us liked the relationship escalator or feeling obligated to anything. What we wanted defied existing labels, so we settled on "partner" and "My Ember" and "My Corry".

Polycule Bliss

A month later I met Sihaya and we all hit it off great as a group. We spent lots of time together and as individual 1-on-1 relationships too. I thought this would feel difficult and complicated, but it wasn't at all. Everyone was so mutually respectful, communicative, and loving. Better than I could've ever imagined.

My time with Ember, Bethany, and Sihaya was idyllic. It was the happiest I'd ever been in a relationship dynamic yet.

I have to say "yet" here to remind myself I can be this happy again. Right now it's hard to imagine it, but I know I have to keep myself open to the possibility.

Ember is Born

Ember exploded from her egg in a manner that left everyone in awe.

Bethany was the only one present with her when it happened after a scary evening fighting for a bed in a horrible ER. Bethany even needed to hide in a restroom to avoid security kicking her out and separating them -- an unimaginably cruel thing to try and take away the only person able to advocate for a patient who can't speak or breathe well.

Ember's mom was in town to help her get through and recover from surgery, so we all got to meet her and immediately bonded over our shared love. Her surgery went well at first, but complications arose a week later. Ember woke up with bleeding and trouble breathing. Her mom told us what was going on and then stayed at Ember's apartment to care for the dogs while Bethany took her to the emergency room in SF.

Sihaya and I weren't allowed to visit, but we swung by to drop off supplies for them to stay the night. We hung out at a nearby gay bar to try and relax and await a call if they needed anything else. We were unable to distract ourselves from worrying.

Eventually they secured a bed and after hours in excruciating pain and labored breathing they finally got Ember some morphine. Within a few minutes of it kicking in and her feeling at peace in her breathing and nerves. Once she realized she was going to survive...

The Phoenix Rises

Bethany describes a moment when the old version of her died and Ember immediately awoke. She moved differently, it was noticeable even in the first few seconds.

Oh my god. Am I a girl?

Ember

You are if you say you are.

Bethany

I imagine Ember slowly descended from levitating several feet off the hospital floor and then laid back in her hospital bed to finally rest.

That Night

After awakening, Bethany tried to keep Ember from talking too much, but she was so excited -- as excited as someone on morphine who hadn't slept in 36 hours could be. She was immediately a new person. She asked for her phone so she could play "I Don't Want to Miss a Thing" by Aerosmith and lip-synced it with tears in her closed eyes.

When Ember got home from the hospital after breaking out of her egg, she wasn't sure when she'd be ready to tell her. But they watched K-Pop Demon Hunters together and she couldn't contain it any longer. Ember's mom was immediately accepting and wonderful about the revelation. They talked about what she wanted her new name to be and how she could honor her family.

It was a beautiful night I only heard about secondhand, of course, so I won't detail more.

The Emberwarming

Once Ember emerged and resumed texting our group chat again, she requested our aid in her transformation. She was about to take 3 weeks to recover from surgery and planned to return to work fully transformed. The entire polycule immediately got to work.

I started briefing her on all the health insurance hoops she'd need to jump through to get all the gender-affirming care she wanted. She knew how much work I went through for mine, and I aimed to shorten her delays as much as possible.

Bethany began researching wigs and consulting with her about what she wanted. The entire polycule and several friends created a group chat for the Emberwarming. We shared makeup tips, skincare routines, fashion advice, and exchanged all the gender-bendy memes.

I made a spreadsheet of all her measurements she could reference when ordering clothing online with their size charts. I had one for myself when I started transitioning, and Bethany helped us get all her measurements on her own version.

We arranged a clothing swap, multiple Sephora runs, makeovers, and wig fittings. She somehow survived a waxing regimen that would kill any mere human.

She gave her workplace a heads up that she was coming back with a new name and pronouns. From what I heard, they did a great job respecting her wishes and supporting her in a professional manner.

This beautiful person who shied away from the front-end of cameras most of her life was now sending us selfies! 💖

Challenges

She had growing pains. Moments of impostor syndrome, insecurity, discrimination, and street harassment. Things all of us had already experienced in our own journeys living our truth, but she was experiencing as fresh traumas. There were times when she wouldn't leave her apartment. When she couldn't feel comfortable in either "boy mode" or in her newer expression.

Within the first couple weeks of her coming to work as herself, a random woman on the street shouted transphobic comments at her. Just a shitty human being verbally abusing a stranger who's done nothing to deserve it. This experience chilled her for months. She spiraled back into insecurity and shame she didn't deserve. She didn't feel safe going into the office for weeks after, and luckily her kind boss allowed her to work from home during that time -- despite Gavin Newsom's asshat return to office mandates.

I remember the first time I got random street harassment for being queer. I was in elementary school, and I didn't even know what the words they shouted at me meant. For years after, I was beaten while being called those words I still didn't really understand. No internet yet. No one told me anything except it meant I like boys in a romantic way, which I didn't. I didn't like anyone yet. It confounded me.

Ember went through similar experiences when she was young. We talked about them a lot.

Victimization

I also remember the first time it happened to me after coming out. How different it felt to not be ignorant. Or to be able to deflect it when I still thought I was cis.

What silly misguided people those were, assaulting me thinking I'm not a normal straight cis white man! Well, joke's on those guys! They got the wrong guy!

My Imagination, ages 14 - 38

But the first time someone threw an empty soda can at my head and called me a faggot from their passing car after coming out wasn't deflectable. I am no longer an ally, I am the object of their hate. It struck me much differently than when I'd put myself between them and their targets. Ember went through a similar shake-up.

Bethany, her, and I talked about it frequently. That horrible woman probably hasn't thought about it again once since that day, but I'm writing about it now. It, like much of Ember and my mutual experiences with bigotry, is fucking unfair.

Recovering

Eventually, she got the courage back up to return to her office. Her coworkers helped rebuild her confidence. Over the most recent months, working with us on going out as her full self outside work hours helped build her up too. Refining her casual styling helped. Being femme without her wig and still feeling good helped. She even went to her sister's wedding in her glorious smooth glory.

Soon after returning from the wedding, Bethany helped find her a high-quality wig she could afford. They traveled all over the Bay Area to track down the right one from the right place. And when she finally found it, Bethany customized it for her with her hair expertise, cutting it to frame her face perfectly. She really glowed in that look, and it was much more comfortable to wear all day than the cheaper ones she had before.

She was bogged down by how much effort and expense it took to feel like herself. Something I've come to call the blue, white, and pink tax. The pink tax cis women experience combined with the additional effort it takes a trans woman to get to where cis women start when they wake up each morning. HRT, hair removal, constantly contentious medical appointments, surgeries, and more. She was at the hardest part of that journey, and I regularly reminded her it gets easier and better.

Each of these careful steps built her confidence and gave her more freedom. She'd continued to pay her 🏳️‍⚧️ tax begrudgingly, but we kept telling her how it gets easier, cheaper, and more affirming with time.

Ember's New Found Confidence

Ember and I continued our hard-to-explain combination of romantic friendship, friends with benefits, but also committed relationship. It was wonderful, especially as she continued to come into her new unmasked identity. I loved her true self even more than her egg form. She was all the best parts of her always-amazing-self but with less anxiety, less apprehension, and more swagger.

I reveled in witnessing her experience what I had when I was at the same stages in my transition. I loved being her "trans godmother" the way other friends had done for me when I started.

Ami

She told Bethany and I how she was thinking about asking out this cute person she played D&D with, and we hyped her up to shoot her shot. Luckily, it went great and Ami and Ember went on their first date not long after. We were so happy for her and amazed at how proactive she was being. Less like the shy Chopper-like character she related to, and more her own new thing.

I got to meet Ami in person the first time a few months ago at a small pool party we hosted. She was great, as expected. Our little polycule family was extending beautifully.

Ember had excellent taste in people, as always. All the connections I've met since her passing have only confirmed that over and over again.

Getting to Know the True Ember

Ember's friends all knew who she truly was inside. Sooner and more deeply than she'd even allow herself to know for a long time. It was easier for us to adjust to her rebirth than it was for her. She was accepted by her chosen family immediately. She was more worried about her family, coworkers, and the public.

At this stage, I felt more like her older sister than a romantic partner. I focused on nurturing her without giving her unrealistic expectations for the road ahead. We commiserated about navigating health insurance, being disrespected at work as misogyny hit her for the first time, and the terrible dysphoria that hits when our 5 o'clock shadow starts appearing each day or as we reach the last few days between waxing appointments.

But it was also beautiful. I love mentoring. It reminds me of how far I've come myself. I got to witness the incredible first euphoric moments, like when Bethany helped her find her first wig. When we'd all do our makeup together before karaoke nights or parties. Her tears of happiness felt so familiar to me and let me experience a small version of them myself again. No one deserved that euphoria and happiness more than Ember.

Realizing Where Her Fear of Death Came From

A few weeks after her big medical scare and awakening, her chosen family were all hanging out at Public Market. She talked about something her therapist had talked to her about shortly before her surgery. Something like:

Why do you think you fear death so much?

Therapist

I'm not sure. I just hate the idea that I'm running out of time.

Ember

Running out of time... to do what?

Therapist

And that was a moment of realization for Ember. She feared running out of time to live as her true self.

Before this, many of her fantasies about future life -- retirement to Santa Fe where she could raise a ranch full of ornery chihuahuas and build all her stockpiled Gundam kits in peace -- included solitude.

I surmised (never confirmed with her) that some of that was because she'd be away from judgment for being herself. But her plans were starting to change to include us in the mix too. I loved the idea of all of us growing into old ladies and enbies together.

I'm glad she got 8 months of living truly. It's not enough, not by a longshot, but I think it's more than she expected to get. She lived the hell out of those 8 months. I'm blessed to have witnessed it.

Ways She Showed Her Love to Me

Overlapping all of this, because life never stands still, were moments of wonderful generous connection. I could never recall everything she did for me in a blog post, but I'll share highlights.

Ember Takes Care of Me After Surgery

I got top surgery in late 2025, and Ember visited me and did supply drops as she loved to do.

She wasn't always comfortable with long hang outs when someone was sick, but she always showed us she cared. It was another thing I loved about her: She knew what she was comfortable with and never promised more than she could offer. And we never expected more from her than she offered. And she offered plenty.

She did it again this August when I had FFS. When they released me, she came down to San Leandro to pick us up from the hospital. She drove all over the Bay Area to find the exact number of the specific type of bandages we needed for my daily re-wraps. Everyone in my community played different roles based on their strengths and love languages, and Ember's was often the fetch quest.

Ember Comforts Me During My Breakup

While I was recovering from surgery, I had my heart broken. I couldn't easily leave my home, couldn't drown my sorrows, and unexpectedly lost a big chunk of my support system in the process.

Ember came over when I asked her, despite her being skeptical she anything to offer. She wasn't sure how she could help, and underestimated how insightful and comforting she could be.

I told her what happened, and the shock on her face at only the first part of it validated my feelings so fully. Her expressions were always honest, especially when she was trying to hide them for politeness. I loved that she couldn't. My mind was trying to minimize my pain to keep the relationship I'd loved for the year before. Ember was one of the confidants who helped me honor my own feelings. She didn't tell me I should do anything. She just listened and asked questions to help herself understand. And through her I understood better too.

She was amazing like that.

Established Relationship Energy (ERE)

For me, Ember went from "a really cool online friend" to "my cuddly best friend" to "the first man I ever loved" to "my fiercely protected little sister" and then back to a romantic relationship again as she settled into her womanhood. It was complicated and unique for both of us, which is why the only labels we ever put on each other was "My Ember" and "My Corry".

In the most recent months, we warmed back up to our romantic chemistry again. The mentor/mentee relationship I felt I had settled down as her confidence in herself grew. While my romantic relationship with her may have lagged behind her and Bethany's in some ways, we were still growing together. We were planning a future.

Ember Volunteers for my Campaign

From the moment I decided to run, Ember's been with me. She never wanted to be visible or prominent and her skeleton would've jumped out of her body and ran away if I asked her to knock on doors with me, but she helped guide my policy. She used her knowledge of city budgets and what workers have to actually do behind the scenes to help me craft messaging. She fed me accurate useful stats I could use in my visualizations and campaigning. She was a big part of the brain trust behind my entire run.

When we reached October and hit the critical (from what I was advised) "Yard Signs" stage of the election, she swooped in to almost entirely fund our yard sign printing. She said she'd been meaning to donate for months but kept forgetting, but she was right on time in my eyes.

When she died two days before the election, I no longer cared about the results. She was one of the main people I was doing it for. When she told me she was proud of me 🥹, it helped me keep pushing through the difficult process. 😩

I could feel how disappointed she was when I didn't win, even though I was relieved. I can't imagine how I'd be able to serve my city in the state I'm in right now. We'll see how she is in a couple years. If I feel her pushing me to try again, I might have to. It'll be much tougher without her here, though.

Ember & Bethany Deepen Their Love

I can only speak to their relationship secondhand, of course, but it was magical. When we all hung out together as a polycule or as our own little triad, it was euphoric. I'd never seen either of them so happy than when we were all together.

After we got through Ember's surgery recovery, Bethany had really deepened her love for Ember. Being afraid to lose someone and going through something like that together awoke her awareness about how serious their relationship actually was, and had been even before those weeks. They'd been in love since before they first kissed, but every date they had seemed to level them up drastically.

Ember asked us something like:

I don't want to steal your thunder or anything, but I'd like to ask Bethany to marry me. You know, not legally since y'all are already married, but ...

Ember

I learned later that she'd already asked one-on-one and Bethany -- of course -- said yes. She just wanted me to feel included and invite me to celebrate with them.

I was so happy for them, I suggested a wedding cake that'd be perfect for these two rice fanatics.

Seriously, the way these two freaks talked about how much they love rice should be studied in a lab somewhere.

Our Last Night

We obviously had no idea this would be our last night together.

It was Halloween and it was a Saturday, which is a miraculous combination. Ember never wanted to go out on work nights, which is why we moved our karaoke nights from Wednesdays to Saturdays in the first place. This year karaoke felt like the perfect way to celebrate the holiday.

One Piece

In the years leading up to this night, Ember had been getting all of us into One Piece, her favorite anime and perhaps her favorite manga and show in general too. The show is enormous, spanning 27 years and nearly 1200 episodes and counting. It's a hard sell to start from episode 1, but she eventually got me into it. I've been slowly catching up and really enjoying it.

I'm currently on episode 183, and now it seems I must finish the other 1,000. I'll feel Ember sitting next to me on the couch with her head on my shoulder the entire time.

When the live action Netflix show aired, it was fantastic. It was consolidated and modernized in a way that allowed Bethany (who has a lot less time to watch TV than geeks with desk jobs) to get into it too. They'd frequently watch together on date nights.

Ember was so happy to cross-pollinate her special interests to us, and we loved the show too. She was right about a lot of stuff.

So this year we all decided to dress up as One Piece characters.

Our Costumes

I dressed as a female version of the main character Luffy, the unlikely captain who fights for his friends , his dreams, and his friends' dreams no matter how hopeless it seems. One Piece has hundreds of interesting characters and I don't usually like the protagonist of most shows, but every time I did an online quiz about which character I am, Luffy was the answer. And it was starting to make sense.

Bethany dressed as Nami, the fiercely independent badass navigator with a shady past. Nami's story also resonates a lot with Bethany in ways I wouldn't be the best person to properly describe.

Ember's favorite character is Chopper, a blue-nosed reindeer creature who's shy and anxious about humans. He isn't comfortable with being complimented and his character growth is all about learning to believe in himself as much as his friends Luffy, Nami, and the rest of the crew believe in him.

The Night Begins

As usual, we all met at Bethany's apartment to get ready. This time, in addition to our regular makeup and outfits we all lend each other and help with, we had costumes too! Ember used some of Bethany's makeup and my bright blue lipstick from a previous costume to nail Chopper's iconic blue nose.
