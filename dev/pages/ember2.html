After Karaoke

These were the worst 4 5 hours of my life, which isn't a small statement. Skip past the hospital part if you like.

After the cheering finally faded, Ember sipped some water and went outside to get fresh air with Bethany. I stayed at our table and watched our purses as I waited to sing my final song a few spots later in the queue. After a few minutes, they came back inside and Ember asked if we could head out. I was pretty tired and not that emotionally attached to my next song, so I canceled it as Bethany called our Lyft.

As we packed our stuff and paid our tab, I noticed Ember taking off her costume and sweating. It was crowded in the bar and she just did an intense song. I wasn't too concerned until she took off her wig -- she never did that in public. We asked how she was doing. She said she was okay and just wanted to go home.

Sunday, November 1st

We got into our Lyft just after the clock turned past midnight.

Shortly after we entered the car, Bethany noticed Ember's breathing was labored. She said her chest hurt and we got scared. Bethany asked if she wanted to go to the ER and Ember didn't want to. I insisted that breathing problems aren't anything to mess around about, and as I said that our Lyft driver chimed in and said the same thing. He said he was a nurse and started driving us immediately to the ER at Kaiser Medical Center in Oakland. As he did, he instructed Bethany on how to update the route on the app so he wouldn't get in trouble for taking us somewhere else.

Ember said something about not having Kaiser insurance, and we told her to focus on her breathing. This is an emergency, so we go to the closest place and figure out insurance later.

The Hospital

I leapt out of the car and ran inside to fetch a wheelchair for Ember. The guards at the front waved me past the metal detectors when I said we had a breathing issue. When I rolled back to the car, Ember could barely get out of her seat. Bethany helped me maneuver her into the wheelchair and I pushed her as fast as I could over the bumpy sidewalk into the emergency entrance.

At the front desk, we told them about her chest pains and breathing. They took her blood pressure and put the thing on her finger to measure vitals. The nurse reassured her that she was alright. Her blood oxygen levels were good and her heart rate was okay. She said it was probably just an anxiety attack, and instructed Ember to breathe deep and slow.

Bethany stayed with her while I gave the nurse her information. She'd given me her wallet to hold onto before we arrived at karaoke because I was the one with the biggest purse that night. I showed her ID and told her about her allergies and medications as best as I could remember them.

They pulled her back to the next room for an EKG and other standard tests and said only one of us could go with her. Bethany stayed and held her. I finished passing information to the front desk nurse before she directed me to sit nearby and wait, my arms full of Ember's Halloween costume.

Code

Within a minute or two of my butt hitting the seat, alarms went off. I heard a nurse yell "STEMI!" and the entire room cleared of staff to run into where Ember and Bethany were.

This was far from my first time in an ER. I'd heard these alarms before, so it took a moment to realize it wasn't background noise this time. I got up and walked over to peek around the corner. The room was empty and I saw Bethany on the other side sitting on a bench in the hallway crying. I ran to her and sat beside her, careful not to bump into any staff running around. The hallway was eerily empty after bustling moments earlier.

Bethany told me how soon after they put the EKG sensors on Ember, they shouted STEMI and she collapsed in the chair, nearly falling out of it. Then, in a flash, everyone moved her onto a stretcher and into a back room down the hallway and around the corner from where we now sat.

We were shocked, scared, crying, and holding each other for a long moment.

Then Bethany called Ember's mom. It was after 2am where she was, so her phone was on do not disturb. Even repeated calls and texts couldn't break through. We didn't know if we should tell anyone else before we got through to her, so we kept trying. As Bethany called again, I texted her not-too-detailed summaries as unemotionally as I could muster.

I looked up all the words I heard the nurses using. Namely STEMI, which we learned was "a severe type of heart attack in which there’s a complete blockage in one of your coronary arteries" and in summary of lots of other text: it's one of the most difficult types to survive.

Every couple minutes someone would come back to tell us some combination of "we're doing everything we can" and "there's a lot going on" and offering us water. After a few rounds of those updates, they told Bethany she could come back and be closer to Ember, so she did.

"A Lot Going On"

I watched the nurse walk Bethany down the hall and around the corner and sat by myself on the bench in the eerily quiet hallway. People who were waiting crept around the corner and peeked in at me, probably curious where everyone went. I have no idea what I looked like, but they saw my face and quickly went back to their seats.

I don't know how long I sat there crying, sipping too-cold water out of a plastic cup, and texting Ember's still-asleep mom as I thought of updates. Desperate for something to do to feel less helpless, but too scared to look up any more medical terms or probabilities. Eventually a nurse came to get me and let me join Bethany with Ember. I hoped this meant things were stable.

They weren't.

As I turned the corner, I saw a scene that's now seared in my memory.

One of the large rooms with windows on all sides I'd walked by dozens of times. Only it wasn't empty like usual when I did before. It was loud, brightly-lit, and filled with maybe 12 medical professionals. It looked like a scene from The Pitt, but with 4x as many people inside. I couldn't see Ember at all except for quick glimpses between people as they moved around her.

Bethany was sitting on a chair just outside the left side of the doorway, losing her shit. I hurried over and held her, kneeling on the floor next to her until they got me a chair. A nurse stood beside her, explaining what was going on in real-time as best and gently as she could manage.

Over the top of the crowd a large muscular man in an orange vest was doing the most vigorous chest compressions I'd ever seen. So violent I worried about Ember's pain levels after she wakes up from all this.

8-10 others huddled around him, each moving deliberately and fast. They opened plastic containers for objects I only saw blurs of and tossed the containers behind them into the edges of the glass room. As the minutes went by the pile of plastic grew taller and surrounded them like the edges of a crater.

A woman in the back right corner wearing a suit jacket and holding a clipboard shouted commands, and the doctors shouted responses. It reminded me of working in fast-paced kitchens and answering the expo or chef. The most notable word we kept hearing every minute or two was:

Each time followed by some movement, a few seconds, then:

The first time I heard it felt like being punched in my chest. Bethany reacted too, less sharply, but she felt me too and told me they'd done that several times before I got there. They'd do it at least ten more times as we sat watching together.

It's over... right?

I vaguely remembered CPR classes I took to be a lifeguard for a summer job, where they told us if we couldn't get someone breathing again within 3 minutes, they were gone.

I've since learned that's totally different for non-drowning circumstances, especially cardiac arrest.

Bethany had heard something similar, so we both thought she was gone right then and there. We emotionally fell apart, but the doctors kept trying. Everything swirled around us and blurred with tears. I lost track of everything.

The Social Worker

A new person, a woman maybe ten years older than us, walked up to us and introduced herself as a social worker. She asked us our names and our relation to Ember. Through mad tears, we said "We're both her partners. We're poly."

Her eyes went wide. She seemed legitimately out of her depth with this information, which I thought seemed strange in a busy Oakland hospital in 2026. After a moment of gears turning in her mind, she seemed to shake off her discomfort and said:

And she hugged us both tightly, warmly. One arm around each of us. It was a great hug. She didn't back away until we did -- maybe a full minute later.

We composed ourselves somewhat as the doctors continued to work behind her. She talked about counseling and started giving us information that sounded like she was easing us into the idea of losing Ember. Our minds were already slamming into that wall on our own, so we welcomed her experienced gentle form of delivering the information.

Pulse!

Then, I don't know how long after, I heard them say they got a pulse. A few moments later a doctor came to us to tell us they were working to get her stable enough to move her upstairs to a specialized cardiac unit. He said we weren't in the clear yet, but this was good news.

The social worker pivoted from her previous topic and guided us up to the next floor. She rode the elevator with us and sat us down in a waiting room. The same waiting room Bethany waited in when I got top surgery, and where I waited when she got her hysterectomy. Not a horrible room, but absolutely an anxious room. She asked if we needed anything other than the water she already provided and told us someone would come up soon to let us know she'd been moved to the cardiac unit.

Another Waiting Room

Another family, a couple perhaps in their 50's and a young woman, were already there in the opposite corner. They were as anxious as we were, milling around and asking each other what was taking so long.

Bethany tried Ember's mom again, but still no reply. It was about 1am for us and 3am where she was.

I decided to call Rhys. I left a voicemail and then texted to give them a heads up. They immediately knew something was wrong but also knew from experience that the hospital wouldn't let them in after visiting hours as a non-relative.

When they asked what they could do, I suggested they go take care of Ember's dogs. Bethany and I were doing our best to think of what Ember's top priority would be. What she'd ask us about as soon as she woke up, if we let ourselves be optimistic. There was no question, Shasta and Diablo would be her first concern.

Teamwork

Rhys came to the hospital so I could hand off Ember's apartment key. As they pulled up, I asked if they wanted a hug and they definitely did. I didn't realize until I was giving it that I needed it just as much. We held each other for a long time and ugly cried as I shared the latest information from the doctors. Eventually Rhys departed and took care of the dogs.

I walked back inside and the security guard almost didn't let me back in. I thought of Bethany up in the big empty waiting room by herself and terrified. I remembered how hospital security in San Francisco kept trying to kick Bethany out earlier this year.

I sized the guard up and considered bolting down the hallway to the elevators before he could catch me.

Luckily he let me back in. He had me print a visitor badge, which required a photo. The selfie camera on the iPad abruptly reminded me I was still dressed as Luffy. What a surreal fucking night.

I wouldn't leave Bethany's side again that night.

Back Upstairs

Shortly after I returned, a doctor came out to update us. He was kind and gentle in his speech, but there was no way to say what he needed to say to us without it feeling like Mike Tyson punched us in the gut.

While we were getting her ready to move up to the cardiac unit, we lost her pulse again. They're still trying to get it back right now.

Pulling the tiny glimpse of regained hope out from under us.

We have to start thinking about what Ember would want. At this stage, we can't guarantee she'll live a normal life after this if we can get her through.

After all our talks about immortality, I knew exactly what she'd want. Bethany said they'd had similar conversations too, so we told him: "Ember would want you to try absolutely everything to survive. Even if she'd be just a head in a jar, she'd want to live." Neither of us would want that for ourselves, but we were certain it's what she'd want.

The doctor heard us loud and clear, excused himself, and told us we'd get another update soon.

WTF is Time?

Every minute we waited for each update felt like an hour. I began noting what time everything was happening so I wouldn't angrily bang on the doors and demand an update every 5 minutes.

It only made things worse when daylight savings ticked the clocks back while we were sitting in one of those torturous waiting rooms, forcing us to experience an additional hour of the worst night of our lives.

A doctor came and spoke to the other family. They gathered their things and went inside the back room. Maybe ten minutes later the older man and young woman walked back out and left. I presumed the other woman would stay the night with whoever they were there for.

We were alone in the waiting room. After a few moments the lights turned off, sensing no motion in the room. We didn't mind.

Another Update

Forever later, another nurse came by. She told us they still hadn't stabilized Ember, but we needed to move to another waiting room further upstairs. First, Bethany would need a visitor badge like the one I had to get. So we picked up all our things and navigated the labyrinth of elevators and hallways to do all that.

It was as awful for Bethany as it was for me to take a selfie in these circumstances. This security guard was more tactful and sympathetic, at least.

We arrived in the third waiting room of the night on the seventh floor. It was disorganized, with furniture strewn about chaotically. Smashed up Ritz crackers littered the carpeted floor. The TVs were off and the room was totally silent. We could see a wide view of the City of Oakland from the large windows.

I imagined children playing here, blissfully unaware of what was going on as their parents or guardians sat in the chairs we sat in and disintegrated inside. Running around with Ritz-crumb-coated fingers while the adults bottled up anxiety we had no reason to hide.

Shortly after the second 2am of that night,

A doctor came in and told us that they still haven't been able to keep her stable for more than a few minutes at a time. They told us that even if we instructed them to continue, there's a point when the doctors in the room will have to call it. She can only take so much of the drugs and other interventions before she wouldn't survive them either. I asked if they had any idea how much longer they'd try, and they said within an hour.

They asked if they could get us anything. I asked for gluten free snacks. We hadn't eaten anything in over six hours at this point and had no idea when we'd next get the chance. One of them came back a few minutes later with some pudding cups, juice boxes, and not-gluten-free graham crackers. They apologized they didn't have much to offer at this time of night. We were grateful to have something and the little apple juice box felt suitable to me in the environment.

At some point during all this Rhys had gotten back to us. They got to the apartment and the dogs were okay. They attempted to get through to Ember's brother via Facebook, hoping he could call their mom and break through her do not disturb, but hadn't heard back yet.

Yet Another Update

Around 2:50am two doctors came in without the cheerful expressions I'd expect from people who'd just miraculously saved a life.

They told us the news and gave us some details I don't remember to basically summarize as: "We really did try absolutely everything."

Bethany asked if we could see her. The thought terrified me for a moment. They said they'd check and let us know. Then they left us there to break down.

And we did.

After

I texted Rhys with the worst news I've ever had to deliver. They asked if they could see her, but the hospital wouldn't allow it. They weren't even sure if Bethany and I'd be allowed to.

We couldn't bring ourselves to tell her mom via text, so we kept trying to call her but failed. The chat history between us dropped off after the last dire update. The silence beneath was deafening and horrifically obvious. I dreaded the thought of her waking up to those messages, then calling us while we were asleep and unable to immediately explain.

A phone rang.

Not mine or Bethany's, but Ember's. We couldn't unlock it, but we could answer the incoming call. It was her brother.

Bethany picked up and I just froze. I had no idea what I could possibly say if it were me on that line. I'd never met him. Bethany only spoke to him once or twice over speakerphone before. I couldn't hear what he said, but the first thing Bethany could muster through shuddering lips was "Ember's dead."

It hit me and seemed to hit Bethany like a ton of bricks to say it out loud for the first time. After a moment she continued "We're at the hospital. The doctors just told us." and more information about where we were and what happened.

He didn't believe her. She asked for his phone number so she can keep in touch with him, but he refused to give it to her. She asked him to call their mom because we couldn't get through to her and he had special privileges to break through her do not disturb. He refused. He said "I don't know you." Bethany wept and tried to reason with him. Eventually, she screamed "My girlfriend just died, and I just need your fucking phone number so we can figure things out!" He hung up and we couldn't call him back.

Bethany broke, again. I was still frozen. I rationalized: he was in shock, just like we were. But it's not like we'd had much more time to process it either. He called less than 10 minutes after she died. Less than 3 minutes after the doctors walked away. It was a second fresh wound on top of an already grievous one.

Surreal

We sat there and cried. It didn't feel real. It wasn't possible. We were all supposed to be at home sleeping. Ember was supposed to wake up in a few hours to leave for her road trip down to Joshua Tree with her dogs. Bethany and I were going to sleep in and have our first relaxing do-nothing day together since my campaign began in July.

What horrible nightmare alternate reality were we in? When would we wake up?

We didn't know what to do. I wanted to get the fuck out of there -- I hate hospitals, even more now. Bethany wanted to see her if we could. I didn't know if I could do it. I was in both freeze and flight mode at the same time, but freeze was winning.

Doctors Return

They came back and told us we'd get a chance to see her. My heart sank. I wanted the universe to make the decision for me that I couldn't see her, but now I'd have to decide myself. I was afraid I'd lose it if I saw her. That it'd make it real.

Bethany was grateful. I told her I'd go in with her and hold her hand, but I may not look. I might not open my eyes. She said that was okay, and thanked me for going with her.

They needed time to prepare her for viewing and bring her to the right floor. We didn't know how long. We felt like we'd been there for a week already.

Rhys came back to the hospital around 3:30 to give Bethany and I a ride home. While we were viewing her, Rhys had a chance to connect with Ember's brother via Facebook. They confirmed he was still in shock, but at least we were able to confirm the bad news. He still didn't trust us though. I don't know why he thought we'd lie, but I supposed I might choose any story other than the one I was telling too.

Viewing

An eternity later, they finally came in to tell us we could see her. They gave us directions to the right elevator, floor, and hallways. I was terrified, holding Bethany's hand just as much for her support as I was to support her. Two people ready to collapse leaning on each other.

We entered the room as a doctor was leaving. He briefly said "Sorry for your loss" and bowed his head slightly as he gave us privacy. I kept my eyes on the tile floor as we passed the curtain and walked around the side of the hospital bed.

She was silent. Bethany's cries were all I could hear as her hand let go of mine to hold Ember's left hand.

I steadied myself on the bed's railing and allowed my eyes to drift up slowly. Her nails were still immaculately painted. As I got the courage to look at her face, I saw she was still wearing her Chopper makeup. My blue lipstick bright as it was when we happily sang and got ready in Bethany's apartment a lifetime ago.

This was the most peaceful I'd ever seen her. No troubled breathing. A fully relaxed expression on her face. My mind wanted to invent a conspiracy theory that it wasn't really her. I didn't let it.

Bethany kissed her forehead and cheek. I couldn't bring myself to touch her. I felt on the edge of collapse as-is.

After a few minutes, Bethany took my hand again and said she was ready to go.

We walked out of the room and the doctor again gave condolences. We asked what was next, and he said the coroner had to come in. That we didn't need to do anything else. They already had Ember's mom's information and would contact her exclusively as the next-of-kin, so we weren't even allowed to know much more.

He said we should get home and try to sleep, and confirmed we had a ride. We could call them anytime if we had questions tomorrow. Then he directed us to the elevator.

Exit

We finally left the hospital around 4:00am. We shambled toward the door, still wearing our Halloween costumes. I was carrying a third Chopper costume in my arms. As we passed, the security guard who almost didn't let me back inside a few hours ago said:

It continued to feel completely surreal. Cruel like a nightmare or a badly-written dark comedy. I muttered a weak "thanks" as the automatic doors slid open and the cold night air swept over us.

We walked up to Rhys in the parking garage as they hung up from calling in sick to work. I put down all Ember's Halloween gear in the car and we cried and hugged together in a trio. Rhys apologized profusely for taking a circuitous route, but I didn't notice.

During the ride, I blurted out an impulse I had almost immediately after I learned she'd died.

Both Rhys and Bethany immediately chimed in and said they had the exact same impulse! We made a pact that we'd all go together and get them done soon.

They dropped us off and we pledged to tell each other whenever we got home safe for the next couple days.

Bethany didn't sleep that night. We just laid in my messy bed with our dogs, weeping.

If you know anything about idiopathic hypersomnia, you know how crazy it is for her to not be able to sleep more than 24 hours after her last dose of medication.

The next several days were a blur.

I lost consciousness for maybe an hour, but didn't feel like I'd rested at all when I opened my eyes around 7am. Bethany wasn't next to me. I could hear her talking on the phone in the next room, and hoped it was with Ember's mom. It wasn't, but I don't remember who it was.

After Bethany hung up, we decided we needed to try and call Ember's mom again. She had to be awake at this point, right? We got her on the line and I barely remember the conversation, but we were all sobbing.

We told her as much of the story as we could.

She told us what the hospital told her:

  • Info about the funeral home and crematorium
  • The death certificate wouldn't be available for 4-6 weeks
  • She was an organ donor

    • Her spine and hip bones will help two people walk again

We talked for a little longer before Ember's mom couldn't speak any longer. She said she was too upset and had to hang up.

We were relieved she knew. She was the person we most needed to tell this horrible news to, and it was by far the hardest of these painful conversations we'd have.

How is the world still turning?

After we made the calls and texts we knew to make, we were no more able to sleep than we'd been all night. The sun was shining through the windows. Rogue and Maupin needed to walk. We needed coffee and breakfast, but we didn't have the energy to make our own.

We took our excited pups to the corner cafe. Rogue jumped up and down as I put her harness and leash on like always, blissfully unaware her Titi Ember was gone. I envied her.

As we sat outside the cafe sipping our coffee and waiting for them to prepare our breakfast order, a neighbor acquaintance called from behind me.

Corry? Hey, is that you? I voted for you! Good luck in the election tomorrow!

While looking through her contacts, we found Pedro (Pete) and Bethany immediately recognized his name. They'd never spoken but Ember told her about him all the time. Unspoken Love

This would keep happening. People I'd never met before or only knew as acquaintances kept telling me amazingly sweet things Ember said about me. I rarely heard her say those things herself. Not verbally, at least. Only with her actions, gestures, affections, and looks.

It's been jarring.

It's made me angry that she didn't tell me herself. I lived with her here under the false impression she was more important to me than I was to her.

It's made me so happy and relieved to be affirmed in her love. I didn't realize how badly I needed verbal validation.

She knew how I felt. I told her all the time, especially because I enjoyed making her blush when she got well-deserved compliments. I'm glad I never held back.

Bethany and I talked about fostering Ember's dogs soon after we knew she was gone. Neither of us have big places or many resources, and we each have a dog with unique social needs already.

We decided to at least try and foster them until a permanent home could be found, but we had to make sure they got along with Rogue and Maupin first.

Introductions

Rhys, Bethany, and I arranged to have them meet at a neutral location in a nearby courtyard. We took many precautions for all the worst scenarios we imagined, and slowly introduced them one at a time. Maupin went first, being the generally more diplomatic of our bunch. Shasta was anxious and Diablo freaked out a little, so Rhys lifted him into air jail so the two blondes could check each other out.

Rogue and I stood back about 30 feet, and she was extremely jealous of her brother. She whimpered and pulled hard on her leash to go join the party. Eventually, it seemed like they were doing okay, so I slowly started letting her walk toward the group. She pulled excitedly until I stopped her just within leash range of Shasta. They sniffed each others' butts in a spiral as Rhys and I circled above them trying to avoid their leashes twisting up. Shortly after, Diablo was lowered to ground level and they all just mixed and swirled with each other to share all the smells and play-fight a little.

It went miraculously fine. They were all fine together. They didn't really substantially bark until the four of them saw another stranger dog on the other side of the courtyard and united as a pack to tell that one off. The three of us all breathed such a sigh of relief because this was the biggest thing we knew Ember would care most about: finding a loving home for her kids. Now we knew they had at least a temporary one.

Transit & Cohabitation

Shasta has severe car trauma. We knew this since Ember adopted her and she had scars on her hands and arms to prove it. This was the next step we needed to overcome to get them home for the night.

We walked the four pups together, their four leashes braiding chaotically the entire time but they continued to get along great. A natural little pack of canines. When we arrived at the car, Rogue, Maupin, and Diablo all hopped into the backseat with relative ease, but Shasta predictably hesitated.

I sat in the rear passenger seat with the three dogs to keep the peace while Bethany planned to drive and I held them away from the door where Bethany and Rhys tried to comfort her and coax her into the vehicle with encouragement and treats. At one point, the three I was holding were staring so hard at Shasta's treats that it looked like a tiny cerberus.

With a little patience and a few treats, Shasta finally hops into the passenger seat in front with Bethany so she could have a first class seat. Once inside, she settled easily and even fell asleep on the ride home.

The Pups' New Home

When they arrived at my home, they all jumped out of the car in a big mess of fur and leashes, resembling a pack already. As we navigated the courtyard of my apartment complex, they sniffed and peed on all the most popular spots. It almost felt like Maupin and Rogue were showing the new kids the ropes.

Once inside my apartment, they ran around and explored excitedly. They found the dog toy crate and food and water bowls. After around 20 minutes of that, they all piled on the couch with us.

Bethany and I finally had a moment of relief. We looked at each other across the couch, covered in dogs, and just said to each other, in an exhausted and still surreal tone:

And we laughed. Then we cried. Ember would be so happy to see them all getting along and having a forever home so quickly. Consistency in their housing was always incredibly important to her. We were so happy to make this possible, so exhausted, and so abyssally sad we had to do it at all.

And then we started adding items to our Chewy cart for our new lives. 💸

My Spiritual Process with Ember

The next morning, Bethany and I were openly weeping at our local coffee shop and I mentioned that I felt Ember's presence draped over my back like a cloak. It felt like she was making me stronger, protecting me like armor. She didn't stop me from being sad. She didn't say anything. She was just there letting me feel my feelings, as she'd done only a few months ago comforting me after a breakup.

It was strange, but comfortable. As we walked back home, I wondered how much of it I imagined due to lack of sleep or grief mirages.

Falling Apart

I was due for ketamine therapy on the Sunday Ember died. I put it off, but the longer I wait after it's due, the worse my mental health baseline gets. So I did it on Tuesday and asked Bethany to lay with me while it happened.

I don't remember much from that experience, but here's some of it.

Over and over, I felt myself become incorporeal. I felt myself fall apart like a sand castle and then dissolve in a sea of tears. Or liquify into a puddle and seep into the fabric and cracks beneath me. As if I'd lost some integral spiritual strength that I was always unconsciously using to hold myself in solid form.

I let myself be nothing for a while. My particles drifted far and wide, becoming passive participants in the universe.

Reincorporating

After indulging in my sadness for however long I needed, I began to feel myself called back together. Slowly, I felt a similar sensation as I did at the coffee shop that morning. A familiar warm and caring presence pulling me back together before remaining between my parts like mortar between stones.

I "awoke" feeling Ember within me. Feeling like I carry her not on my shoulders like armor, but in my bones.

Carrying Her With Me

Now I've taken it upon myself to live in ways Ember would appreciate. Living authentically and uncompromisingly in my identity. Loving Bethany and others as fiercely and sturdily as she did. Eventually watching every episode of One Piece. I'll live the rest of my life feeling her presence alongside me and try not to bore or disappoint her.

Ember's False Family

Some blood relatives who've known less of her and spent less time with her than any of us during the last 10+ years of her life have minimized our grief. Even after Ember formally introduced some of us. Those folks are hurting too right now, so I won't say much more, but it hurt us deeply.

I planned to leave this part out

I planned to leave this part out, to spare my fellow sufferers of grief from being exposed during a difficult time for all of us. But their actions since Ember's death have compounded so badly, with such unnecessary cruelty, that they don't deserve that mercy.

Ember told us about her family many times. We all have families that didn't let us be ourselves. Nearly everyone who's been at her memorials this week. It's not an unusual story. Abuse, narcissism, abandonment, erasure.

There's a reason why many of us live far from our blood relatives. There's also a reason Ember was shy about taking up space and sharing her feelings. Before She Died

After she died, and all her universes began colliding, her people shared stories with each other. Gaps got filled in. Details added. The extent of how many years we had history for expanded.

I believe this is part of what her family was so afraid of when they got here. Us all talking to each other about Ember's story. Her experiences, in her own words, told to people she felt safe around. Everywhere from Puerto Rico to California. From 1978 to 2026.

Ember Would Be Alive in a Just World

I truly believe the compounding stress and healthcare strains of not feeling like she could be her true self until she was in her late 40's shortened her life. In a world where she could've been herself, explored her identity without stigma or abuse, and lived her truth earlier, she'd have had fewer health challenges.

For her last 8 months, she'd been so much happier, healthier, and less anxious. She'd walk around with a calm smile on her face I'd only seen for brief moments before and insist the HRT wasn't doing that much. We'd smile and nod along, plainly seeing it wasn't true at all. Her surgery certainly helped her sleep and breathe better too, but it was much more than that.

It was a lightness that came from no longer needing to hide all the time. Imagine if she felt that lightness since she was a teenager, or maybe even earlier. Puberty is a horribly traumatic experience for trans people, and not knowing acceptance or even understanding what we were feeling only makes it worse. It shoves us into a closet we don't have the key to unlock.

Poly Partner Erasure

Throughout this process, people have treated the three of us in ways that've felt extremely frustrating and hurtful. Each of us have unique connections with Ember of different durations, intensities and styles. All of us are true connected partners.

Some folks mean well and just don't know how to talk to us, like the social worker at the hospital who was trying to comfort us.

Others treated us like we don't matter. If Ember were monogamous, which she hadn't exclusively been for longer than I've been, people would take the grief of her partner much more seriously. But because she had three partners, people often act like we're casual acquaintances. We weren't. We were committed. Our daily routines included each other. We had planned futures together that fell completely apart that night. She effectively has three widows. Each unique. Each valid. Each grieving deeply.
